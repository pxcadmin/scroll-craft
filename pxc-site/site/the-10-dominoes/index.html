The map

Which problems knock the others over?

One missed handoff rarely stays one problem. We map your front desk as ten dominoes and fix the three that are knocking over the rest.

What are the ten front desk dominoes?

The ten places a medical front desk breaks, in the order a patient meets them. Each one leans on the next. Open any of them to see the signs, what it costs, and the first fix.

1. Phones and Callbacks

What it costs. A missed call with no owner is still open at the end of the day, and the patient calls again.

Signs. Voicemail full by mid-morning. The same patient calling twice. Callbacks written on paper.

First fix. One named owner for callbacks, and a set time each day when the list is cleared.

2. Arrival and Check-In

What it costs. Forms, insurance questions and a line at the window all start at the same minute.

Signs. Clipboards at the window. Insurance questions answered in front of the line. Late starts for the first visit.

First fix. Move what can be done before arrival to before arrival, and give the window one job.

3. Wait Time and Flow

What it costs. A delay nobody names feels longer, and the desk absorbs the frustration.

Signs. Patients asking the desk how much longer. No one owning the update. Tension by late morning.

First fix. A simple rule for when a delay is named, who says it, and what they say.

4. Scheduling Integrity

What it costs. Overrides with no rule fill the day past what the team can actually run.

Signs. Double-booked slots. Days that run over. "Just squeeze them in."

First fix. A written override rule: who may, when, and how many a day.

5. No-Shows and Confirmations

What it costs. A reminder goes out, nobody owns the reply, and an open slot sits empty.

Signs. Reminders sent, replies unread. Empty slots found the same day. No waitlist in use.

First fix. One owner for replies, and a short list for filling a slot that opens.

6. Checkout and Follow-Through

What it costs. The visit ends at the door, and the next step is nobody's task.

Signs. Follow-ups not scheduled at checkout. Referrals waiting. Patients calling to ask what happens next.

First fix. A checkout script that ends with a booked next step or a named owner.

7. Admin Requests and Messages

What it costs. Refills, forms, records and portal notes share one pile and no turnaround.

Signs. One inbox for everything. No promised turnaround. Patients calling to check on a form.

First fix. Sort by type, give each type a turnaround, and post the turnaround where the team can see it.

8. Handoffs and Escalation

What it costs. Front-to-clinical handoffs depend on who happens to be nearby.

Signs. "I sent it" with no confirmation. Messages passed in the hallway. Urgent items found late.

First fix. A handoff is done when it is received, not when it is sent. Write down what counts as received.

9. Team Stability and Onboarding

What it costs. A new hire inherits whatever was never written down, and the pattern repeats.

Signs. Training by shadowing only. The manager covering the desk. The same questions from every new hire.

First fix. Write the three routines a new hire needs in week one, and teach from the page.

10. Reputation and Feedback

What it costs. Reviews are often the last domino. Nine others fell first.

Signs. Reviews that mention the phone or the wait, not the care. Complaints heard third-hand.

First fix. Read reviews as a map. Each one usually points at an earlier domino.

Why only three?

Because ten priorities is no priority. Fix the three that are knocking over the rest and the others often stand back up on their own.
